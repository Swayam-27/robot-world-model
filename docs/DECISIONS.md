# Decisions

Every design choice and every experiment change, with the reason. Newest at the bottom. Format: date · decision · why · alternatives considered.

## Starting decisions (2026-11-03)

- **Environment: MuJoCo Pusher-v5** · reads as robotics, contact-rich, 100-step episodes keep rollouts short · alternatives: own maze game (more fun, less robotics), Reacher (too simple, no object).
- **Headline metric: rank correlation of policy rankings** · it is what robotics teams need ("which checkpoint do we deploy?"), mirrors 1X's evaluation result · alternative: pixel error only (doesn't show usefulness).
- **Frames 64x64, 4-frame context** · fits free T4 GPUs, matches DIAMOND's small Atari setup.
- **World model: DIAMOND-style pixel diffusion with EDM** · proven small and playable on one consumer GPU; start from its code, not from scratch.
- **Noise augmentation on context frames, max 0.7** · GameNGen found it essential against drift.
- **State reader instead of true state in the dream** · policy only sees pixels, like a real robot camera; ranking B isolates the reader's error.
- **Policies: SAC via Stable-Baselines3, trained on state, on CPU** · we use RL, we don't research it; saves GPU quota.
- **Eval seeds 10000 to 10049, never in training** · prevents leakage.
- **Compute: free Kaggle GPUs** · no budget; everything kept small.
- **Solo project** (optionally one friend on the policy side only) · every design answer in interviews must be mine.

- **2026-11-04 · Custom fixed camera: distance 1.6, elevation -50°, azimuth 135°, look-at (0.3, -0.3, 0)** (stored as `CAMERA_CONFIG` in `envs/constants.py`) · the default Pusher camera leaves about 70% of the 64x64 frame as black background, and the can is only 2 to 3 pixels; this angle fills the frame with the table and keeps arm, can and goal visible through random episodes · alternatives compared on seed 0 (`outputs/camera_options.png`): straight top-down and 60° tilt (arm leaves the frame at the bottom), side view (arm crowds the left edge).
- **2026-11-04 · All data goes through the `ArmEnv` interface** (`envs/interface.py`: `reset(seed)`, `step(action)`, `frame()`, `state()`), implemented by `PusherEnv` in `envs/pusher.py` · keeps the data source swappable for a phase-2 real arm · alternative: call Gymnasium directly in each script.
- **2026-11-04 · PyTorch port of the faces CNN moved from Step 0 to just before Step 4** · Steps 1 to 3 need no hand-written PyTorch; the port is warm-up for the state reader, which reuses its conv-block design (with flatten instead of global average pooling, since the reader must keep positions) · alternative: do it first as planned (delays MuJoCo work).
- **2026-11-04 · SAC trains through `make_train_env()` in `envs/pusher.py`, which resets every episode from a seed drawn from `TRAIN_SEEDS`** · Stable-Baselines3 needs a standard `gym.Env`, not `ArmEnv`; keeping its construction in `envs/` means `policies/` never calls MuJoCo directly, and the train-seed reset makes "no eval start position seen in training" provable. Scoring and recording still go through `PusherEnv` · alternatives: `gym.make` inside `policies/` (breaks the interface rule), an `ArmEnv`-to-gym adapter (more code for no gain while policies train on true state).
- **2026-11-04 · SAC config: SB3 defaults except `learning_starts` 1,000 (default 100); 300k steps, checkpoint every 20k** · defaults are a known-good baseline for MuJoCo tasks; a few more random steps fill the replay buffer before learning · alternative: tune hyperparameters (not needed: we want a spread of skill, not a record score).
- **2026-11-05 · Second SAC run: 1M steps instead of 300k, everything else identical** (`configs/sac_pusher_1m.yaml`) · `measure_checkpoints` on 20 training seeds showed pushing still improving at 300k (mean final object-goal distance 0.258 m at 20k -> 0.177 m at 200k -> 0.115 ± 0.049 m at 300k; can moved in 100% of episodes from 180k) but the best policy still leaves the can about 11 cm from the goal; the pool needs a top rung that actually reaches it. Fresh run, not resumed: the replay buffer was not saved. Kept on CPU: tiny MLPs and the CPU simulator are the bottleneck, and GPU quota is for Step 4. The 300k run is kept as a record · alternatives: resume from 300k (empty replay buffer, messier), change reward weights (curve was not flat, and that would be a second change). If 1M also flattens far from the goal, reward weights are the next single change.
- **2026-11-06 · Policy pool = option A, 10 brains** (`configs/pool.yaml`): SAC checkpoints 960k, 600k, 400k, 260k, 100k; the 960k brain with Gaussian action noise (std 0.6, 0.8, 1.0, 1.3; noise seeded by start position); a random brain · chosen by screening candidates on the first 50 TRAIN_SEEDS (`policies/pool.py`, never on EVAL_SEEDS): every neighbouring pair is separated by a paired gap > 2 standard errors (1.5 to 2.7 cm), spanning 0.054 to 0.241 m. 960k, not the final checkpoint: the final one was worse and less stable (0.074 ± 0.063 m on 20 seeds). Dropped as near-ties: 20k (= random, neither moves the can), noise 0.3 (= 400k), 140k and 180k (= noise 1.0) · alternative: option B, the same without noise 0.8 (9 brains, 3 noisy copies as in the build guide); A gives a more reliable Spearman and leaves room to hold 2 brains out of the dataset in Step 3.
- **2026-11-06 · Success threshold (pass mark): 3 cm** (`SUCCESS_DISTANCE = 0.03` in `envs/constants.py`) · chosen by eye from `policies/pass_mark_sheet.py` (final frames of 300 training-seed episodes, grouped by final can-to-goal distance): at ~2 cm the can sits centred on the goal, at ~4 cm it has slid toward the rim, so 3 cm marks "clearly on the goal". Chosen before looking at any success rate, never from eval results · alternatives: 5 cm (on the goal but off-centre counts), 7 cm (half on the goal counts). Consequence, accepted: on the eval seeds only sac_960k (30%) and sac_600k (24%) ever succeed, so success rate is a strict secondary number; ranking A and the headline Spearman use mean final distance, which separates all 10 brains.
- **2026-11-06 · Dataset plan (Step 3): record on the laptop, 2,000 episodes, 2 brains held out** (`configs/dataset.yaml`) · recorded brains: sac_960k, sac_600k, sac_260k, sac_960k_n0.6, sac_960k_n1.0, sac_960k_n1.3, sac_100k (200 episodes each) + random (600 episodes, wide action coverage for action-following); every episode starts from a different training seed (TRAIN_SEEDS[100:2100], apart from the seeds used for screening and the pass-mark sheet). **Held out entirely: sac_400k and sac_960k_n0.8** (one plain checkpoint, one noisy copy, both mid-ladder) so Step 8 can test whether the dream ranks policies it never saw · laptop because the checkpoints are local and rendering works on Windows; upload to a private Kaggle dataset afterwards · alternatives: record all 10 (no unseen-policy test), hold out the best/random (too easy or too odd a test), record on Kaggle CPU (SB3 + OpenGL clashes seen in Step 2).
- **2026-11-07 · Pin the simulator versions everywhere: mujoco==3.17.0, gymnasium==1.4.0** (the laptop versions the dataset was recorded with) · the Kaggle replay check (`data/playback.py`) with Kaggle's default mujoco 4.0.0 diverged by up to 34 cm on noisy brains; with 3.17.0 pinned, physics matches within 0.3 mm (careful brains ~1e-7). Rendering still differs: ~3.6% of pixel values differ already at reset (Kaggle Linux EGL vs Windows), up to ~66/255 · alternative: re-record on Kaggle (wastes the verified dataset and the laptop scores).
- **2026-11-07 · All MuJoCo rendering happens on the laptop; Kaggle only does GPU or heavy work on stored data** · Kaggle's renderer draws ~3.6% of pixel values differently from the laptop (DECISIONS above), so mixing laptop-drawn and Kaggle-drawn frames would confound the state reader and ranking B. Concretely: the reader and world model train on Kaggle GPUs from the stored dataset; ranking B (reader in the real simulator) runs on the laptop CPU; dream start frames are rendered on the laptop and uploaded; anything GPU-bound or too heavy for the laptop moves to Kaggle · alternative: render on Kaggle too and accept the 3.6% difference (simpler, but a hidden confound in the results).
- **2026-11-17 · State reader v1 (Step 4b)** (`models/reader.py`, `configs/reader.yaml`): Swayam's CNN (3 conv blocks of 32/64/128, 1x1 conv to 16 channels, flatten 16x8x8, dense 128-64-23; 431,335 params); one example = frames (t-1, t) -> state t for t = 0..100, with frame 0 used twice at t = 0 (what the reader sees right after a reset); MSE on states normalised by the TRAINING split's mean/std, **std floored at 0.01** because the goal never moves (std ~0) and the can height barely does (std 1e-4), which would otherwise blow up the loss on meaningless noise; Adam 1e-3, batch 256, up to 20 epochs, early stopping (patience 5) on validation loss; logged to W&B · alternatives: global average pooling as in the build guide (loses positions, see the 2026-11-04 entry), predict only object/goal (policies need all 23 numbers), drop the constant goal outputs (keeps the reader a drop-in replacement for the true observation).
- **2026-11-19 · Reader v1 is not good enough; diagnose before changing it, on training seeds** · ranking B with reader v1 (`eval/ranking_b.py`, 50 eval seeds): sac_960k 5.8 -> 20.4 cm, all brains 0% success, Spearman(A, B) 0.745 but 8 of 9 neighbour gaps are near-ties, i.e. the order is mostly noise. Plumbing check passed (true state reproduces A exactly), so the drop is the reader. `eval/reader_diagnosis.py` on TRAIN_SEEDS[:50] (sac_960k, true state except one group from the reader): velocities +4.4 ± 0.9 cm, object +2.9 ± 0.9, joint angles +2.7 ± 0.5, fingertip +0.9 ± 0.3, all +13.0 ± 1.1 -> every group contributes and errors compound in closed loop; velocities hurt most. The fix is chosen from these training-seed numbers, never from eval results.
- **2026-11-19 · Reader v2 = v1 with 3 stacked frames (t-2, t-1, t) instead of 2; nothing else changed** (`configs/reader_v2.yaml`, 432,199 params) · the diagnosis showed velocities hurt sac_960k most (+4.4 cm), and two frame differences instead of one give the network more motion signal; this is the build guide's first fallback. At the start of an episode frame 0 fills the missing slots, in training and in closed loop (`FrameWindow`). v1 files and results are kept (`outputs/reader`, `ranking_b.csv`); v2 writes `outputs/reader_v2`, `ranking_b_v2.csv`, `reader_diagnosis_v2.csv` · alternatives: fight overfitting first (weight decay/dropout; next single change if v2 is still weak), 96x96 frames (needs re-recording, and the dream is 64x64), retrain the brains on noisy states (changes ranking A).
- **2026-11-19 · Reader v2 (3 frames) is worse than v1: rejected; v1 stays the base** · validation errors barely moved (velocities 0.533 -> 0.540 rad/s, angles 9.4 -> 9.8 deg, object 1.79 -> 1.62 cm; best epoch 4 instead of 6, i.e. it overfits sooner). Diagnosis on TRAIN_SEEDS[:50] (sac_960k): all from reader +13.0 -> **+17.2 ± 0.9 cm** (0% success); velocities improved (+4.4 -> +3.3) but joint angles (+2.7 -> +6.4) and object (+2.9 -> +5.0) got clearly worse. Ranking B v2 on eval seeds (reported, not used to choose): sac_960k 21.4 cm, every brain 0-2% success, Spearman(A, B) 0.855, but all 9 neighbour gaps are near-ties, so the rise from 0.745 is noise, not progress. Lesson: lower average error on stored photos does not mean better closed-loop behaviour; the brains drive the arm into poses where the reader's errors snowball. Reading (untested): speed may be limited by what 64x64 pixels show (wrist/forearm twists barely change the image), not by the number of frames.
- **2026-11-19 · Reader v3 = v1 + AdamW weight decay 0.05; nothing else changed** (`configs/reader_v3.yaml`, `models/train_reader.py` now uses AdamW with `weight_decay` from the config, default 0; checked that AdamW with 0 gives bit-identical weights to Adam, so v1 and v2 still reproduce) · v1 and v2 both stop improving on validation after 4-6 epochs while training loss keeps falling (overfitting), and weight decay is the simplest standard brake on it. 0.05 is a common AdamW value; one value, not a sweep. Built on v1 (2 frames), the better closed-loop reader. Outputs `outputs/reader_v3`, `ranking_b_v3.csv`, `reader_diagnosis_v3.csv`; judged first on the training-seed diagnosis · alternatives: dropout in the dense head (the other standard brake; next candidate if decay does too little), DAgger-style data (record episodes where brains play through the reader, so it learns the poses it causes; targets the snowball directly but needs new laptop recording and a dataset upload).
- **2026-11-20 · Reader v3 (v1 + AdamW weight decay 0.05) is not good enough either** (W&B rural-disco-3) · validation: best epoch 4, val loss 0.2455 (v1 0.2407, v2 0.2473), velocities 0.539 rad/s, angles 9.7 deg, object 1.82 cm; validation loss now stays flat after epoch 4 instead of rising, but does not go lower. Diagnosis on TRAIN_SEEDS[:50] (sac_960k): all from reader **+16.2 ± 0.9 cm** (v1 +13.0, v2 +17.2); velocities fixed (+0.9, v1 +4.4) but joint angles +5.6, object +4.0, fingertip +2.2. Ranking B v3 on eval seeds (reported, not used to choose): good brains 17.7-22.4 cm, sac_600k 6% success, the rest 0%; Spearman(A, B) 0.855 with 8 of 9 neighbour gaps near-ties. **Conclusion after three readers:** all reach the same validation loss (~0.25) yet fail on different groups in closed loop, so training tweaks only move the damage around. The likely cause is distribution shift: the reader only saw poses reached by brains reading the TRUE state; driven by its own estimates the arm reaches poses it never saw, its errors grow, and they snowball.
- **2026-11-20 · Planned next single change: reader v4 = DAgger-style data (to be detailed before coding)** · record extra episodes on TRAINING seeds where pool brains play through reader v1 while the true state is saved for every frame; retrain with v1's settings on old + new data, so the reader learns the poses it causes itself. This is the standard fix for compounding errors in imitation learning (DAgger, Ross et al. 2011) · alternatives: dropout (a training tweak; v2 and v3 suggest tweaks just move the damage), larger frames (needs re-recording everything, and the dream is 64x64), brains trained on noisy states (changes ranking A).

- **2026-11-21 · Reader v4 (v1 + DAgger round 1) works: it becomes the project's reader** (W&B northern-water-4; best epoch 6, val loss 0.2213 on main+DAgger val, not comparable with v1-v3) · diagnosis on TRAIN_SEEDS[:50] (sac_960k): all from reader **+10.8 ± 1.1 cm** (v1 +13.0, v2 +17.2, v3 +16.2); object +0.7 (v1 +2.9), angles +2.2, velocities +2.3, fingertip +0.6. Because the headline metric is rank correlation, v4 is good enough to move on; further DAgger rounds are the obvious lever if ranking C later suffers from the reader · alternatives: a second DAgger round now (more laptop recording; can come later if needed).
- **2026-11-21 · DIAMOND's U-Net is copied into the repo, not added as a submodule; its Atari demo is skipped** (`models/diamond_blocks.py`, unchanged copy of `src/models/blocks.py` at commit 5bcd159, MIT license in `models/DIAMOND_LICENSE`) · the world-model code is ~480 lines of plain PyTorch; a submodule would drag in DIAMOND's Hydra/Atari setup and its own `Batch` class. Demo skipped: it needs Atari ROMs, Hydra and a pretrained download, and reading the code covered what it would teach · alternatives: submodule (tracks upstream, heavy deps), run the demo on Kaggle (~1 h).
- **2026-11-21 · World-model examples: frames t-3..t + actions t-3..t -> frame t+1, padded at episode start; trained on the main + DAgger datasets** (`models/world_model_data.py`, `configs/world_model_v1.yaml`) · padding (frame 0 repeats, missing actions = 0, same trick as the reader) gives 100 examples per episode and lets a dream start right after a reset. DAgger episodes are real physics too, with more varied arm poses (3,050 episodes, ~305k examples, ~3.8 GB of frames in RAM on Kaggle) · alternatives: drop t < 3 (dreams would need 4 real frames first), main dataset only.
